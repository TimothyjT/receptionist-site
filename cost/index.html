What the market charges

Market summary from FindSkill’s 2026 guide to AI receptionists (checked October 14, 2026; the guide reviews its prices quarterly). Three ways of charging cover most of the market:

Flat monthly rate — about $99 to $299 is typical

One price each month. Easier to budget, and the number to check is what happens at your call volume: ask what counts as a call, and what a busy month does to the bill.

Per minute — roughly $0.08 to $0.50 a minute in the guide’s range

You pay for talk time. Short calls stay cheap. Long calls, hold time, and a busy week add up in ways that are hard to predict before the first invoice.

Per call — roughly $1 to $10 a call in the guide’s range

You pay each time the phone is answered. Simple to count, but a week of wrong numbers and repeat callers still counts.

Source: FindSkill, “What Is an AI Receptionist? A 2026 Guide for Small Business”. Ranges are that guide’s market summary, not our prices and not a quote for your shop.

How to compare without getting surprised

  1. Work out a normal month first. Count the calls you miss in a week — nights, weekends, and while you are on a job. Price every option against that month, not against its first tier.

  2. Ask what counts. Under per-minute and per-call plans, ask whether spam calls, wrong numbers, and hold time are billed. Those minutes are the surprise on most first invoices. Ask a flat rate the same question — what a busy month does to the bill. Our answer is printed in full in the price box above.

  3. Ask what happens on a hard call. A burst pipe at 9:40 PM should reach a person. If a service cannot hand a call to you by a rule you set, its price is not the whole story.

Do the same math we ask owners to do for missed calls: calls you miss in a week × 4.33 weeks in a month × your average job ticket × the share you would have won. If that monthly number comes out under $199, we will tell you not to buy this.

Why do prices range from $25 to $300 or more a month?

Sellers charge in different ways. Some charge a flat monthly rate, some charge per minute of talk time, and some charge per call. A low starting price under a per-minute or per-call plan can cost more than a flat rate once real calls come in, so compare what a normal month of your calls would cost, not the starting price.
