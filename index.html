Sample call · fictional shop

Booking a water heater swap

Listen for the read-back. She spells the caller's name back to him and confirms his number before the booking counts.

Read the transcript

Willa: Bluebonnet Plumbing, this is Willa. How can I help you?

Caller: Hey. Our water heater's leaking all over the garage floor. I need somebody to come look at it.

Willa: I'm sorry you're dealing with that. Is it leaking bad enough to be an emergency, or can it wait for a booked visit tomorrow or the next day?

Caller: It's a slow drip into a pan right now. A day or two is fine.

Willa: All right. A tech can come Thursday at 9 in the morning, or Friday at 1. The visit is seventy-nine dollars, and if you have us do the repair, that fee comes off the bill.

Caller: Thursday at nine works.

Willa: Thursday at 9 AM, booked. Can I get your name?

Caller: Mark Delgado.

Willa: Thanks, Mark. Let me spell that back: Delgado, D-E-L-G-A-D-O. Did I get that right?

Caller: That's right.

Willa: And the best number for the tech to reach you — is it the one you're calling from, ending in 0148?

Caller: Yep, that's my cell.

Willa: You're all set for Thursday at 9. You'll get a text confirming it, and the tech will call when he's on his way. Anything else I can do?

Caller: Nope, that's it. Thanks.

Sample call · fictional shop

The 8 AM slot that's already taken

Listen for what she does not do: invent a time. Saturday morning is full, she says so, and she picks the conversation back up after the caller cuts in.

Read the transcript

Willa: Bluebonnet Plumbing, this is Willa. How can I help you?

Caller: Hi, I need a plumber Saturday morning, early. Like 8 o'clock?

Willa: Let me check Saturday morning for you—

Caller: —because we've got family coming in at noon, so it really has to be the morning.

Willa: Got it, morning it has to be. Saturday at 8 is already booked, and 10 is too. I won't put you down for a time that isn't real. What I do have: Friday at 4:30 in the afternoon, or Monday at 8 sharp.

Caller: Hmm. Friday at 4:30 — that actually works better. Let's do that.

Willa: Friday at 4:30, booked. Your name, please?

Caller: Priya Nair.

Willa: Priya, let me spell your last name back: Nair, N-A-I-R. Correct?

Caller: Correct.

Willa: Done. You'll get a text confirming Friday at 4:30, and if a Saturday morning slot opens up, I'll call you before I offer it to anyone else.

Caller: That would be great. Thanks!

Sample call · fictional shop

A burst pipe at 9:40 PM

Listen for the order of events. The emergency does not sit in a queue: she marks it urgent, texts the owner, tries his phone, and promises a call back from her if he does not pick up.

Read the transcript

Willa: Bluebonnet Plumbing, this is Willa. How can I help you?

Caller: A pipe just burst under my kitchen sink and there's water everywhere—

Willa: Okay, stay with me. First thing: do you know where your main water shutoff is? It's usually a valve near the street or by the water heater.

Caller: I think so. By the garage?

Willa: That's the one. If you can turn it, do that now — it'll stop the water while we get help moving. Can I get your name?

Caller: Jen Okafor.

Willa: Jen, I'm marking this an emergency. I'm texting the owner right now and trying his phone. If he doesn't pick up in the next few minutes, I will call you back myself and tell you the next step. You will not be left waiting without hearing from us.

Caller: Okay. Okay, thank you.

Willa: The message is sent, with your number on it. Keep your phone with you, Jen.

Sample call · fictional shop

The question she won't answer

Listen for the permit cost. It is not one of her facts, so she says that plainly and offers a callback with the real number instead of guessing.

Read the transcript

Willa: Bluebonnet Plumbing, this is Willa. How can I help you?

Caller: Yeah, two questions. Do you install tankless water heaters, and what's the city permit cost on something like that?

Willa: We do install tankless units. A standard install starts at twenty-one hundred dollars, and that includes the unit.

Caller: And the permit?

Willa: That's the part I don't have in front of me, and I'd rather not guess at a number you'll hold us to. Here's what I can do: I can have the owner call you back today with the exact permit cost. Or I can book the seventy-nine dollar visit, and he'll bring the number with him.

Caller: Just have him call me.

Willa: Done. I've noted both questions, the tankless install and the permit cost, and he'll call you back today at this number. Anything else?

Caller: No, that covers it.

Who is AI Phone Receptionist for — and who is it not for?

It’s for you if…

  • You miss calls all day because you’re out on jobs.
  • After-hours callers reach your voicemail and never call back.
  • You run a one- or two-person shop with no one free to answer the phone.

It’s not for you if…

  • You already have a full-time receptionist answering every call.
  • You need emergency dispatch calls handled with no human involved. Real emergencies and hard calls always go to a person.
